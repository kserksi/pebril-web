Why Pebril Exists

An afternoon of looking for a photograph, and a pebble that wants to stay in the river.

I write, and I take photographs. The album grows a little thicker every year, and finding one particular picture in it gets a little harder.

Most albums sort by folder and by date, and they do it neatly. They remember that the photograph exists. They do not remember that mountain, or the mist that afternoon.

That part I have to carry myself.

I tried a few open-source albums too. Some of them ask you to settle a server, a database and a long list of settings first, before you are allowed to see a single photograph. Some were young, and went quiet at some version, and never woke up again. The last one I used could not keep its breath when I moved too many pictures in at once.

There was no grand plan in that moment. There was only one stubborn question:

Why should keeping a photograph be this hard?

Pebril grew out of that question. It does not try to prove that one language is better, and it does not want to deny what others have already built. It only hopes to give people looking for a home for their pictures one more place that might last.

A pebble

The name comes from two things: a pebble, and a river.

Time is a long river. Photographs are the moments we want to keep. What Pebril wants to be is a small pebble at the bottom of that river — a place where an afternoon can rest for a while, and still be found again many years later.

The lamp is the other thing. A lamp in the night changes shape, colour, and the direction its light comes from; the person it lights is always the same.

Code will be rewritten. The interface will be replaced. The people who keep it will change. One thing should not change: it has to be honest with the people who use it.

What we believe

Respect different choices. Different technologies suit different situations. Pebril should be chosen for its own design and for the way it behaves in practice, not because someone else’s choice was said to be wrong.

Light does not mean feature-poor. Light means fewer unnecessary dependencies, a little less taken from the machine, a little less to keep an eye on. Complexity that developers can carry themselves should not be handed to the people who use the product.

Images come first. Uploading, converting, searching, managing, sharing — each of these should answer a real need. A photograph should not become an accessory of a larger system.

Reliable. A photograph is not only a file. It holds a piece of memory that cannot be replaced. Wholeness, access, backup, and the way back should all be taken seriously. Failures will happen; at the very least they should be visible, their effect should have edges, and a way back should stay open.

Freedom with boundaries. The interface, the way it is deployed, the settings, the places it can be extended — all of these should offer real choices. But identity, permissions, and the integrity and safety of data cannot be let go simply because something is called open. Openness is freedom inside rules that hold.

Community before the individual. A project starts with one person, but it should not belong to one person’s imagination forever. When others are willing to take it on and to be responsible for it, the weight of deciding should pass to them, slowly.

What we do not intend to do

Pebril does not need to be the only image management tool. It does not need the most users, and it does not need to stay loyal forever to one language, one framework, or one platform.

It may change with technology, be redesigned by its community, and grow into something I did not imagine when I began.

Money is not the first priority now. If support, hosting, help with deployment, or paid services come later, they will not be funded by weakening the open-source core, closing the data format, restricting the interface, or making it harder to leave.

In the end

I hope that many years from now, when someone wants to keep, manage, and find their own pictures, they will still remember that an open-source project called Pebril is there to choose.

It does not need to belong to any one person. It does not need to become what anyone once prescribed.

A pebble to hold a memory. A small light to guide us home.

May Pebril begin as a small light, and grow freely.

What is written here is intent, not features already delivered. What works today, what is decided, and what is still being explored will be told separately in later notes.
